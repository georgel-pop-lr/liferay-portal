# 601: Consolidate Parallel Test Methods

Fold parallel `@Test` methods into one `@Test` that calls a `_<testMethodName>` helper per case. Two shapes call for it.

The first is several `@Test` methods that do nothing but call the same assertion with a different input, trivial one liners that differ only by an argument. Inline the cases as comment separated sections when the setup is trivial, or factor a helper and call it once per case when the setup is nontrivial and would otherwise be repeated.

The second is sibling scenario methods that test the same method of the subject and each rebuild the same fixture. Their names already share the `test<MethodUnderTest>` stem that rule 603 gives them, so they collapse into one `@Test` named after that method alone, which builds the fixture once and passes it to a `_test<MethodUnderTest><Scenario>` helper per scenario. Keep each scenario's name intact on its helper; the naming chain is the one rule 605 uses, `_testGetFooWithNullBar` called from `testGetFoo`.

This is a matter of taste, not an absolute, and the codebase leans the other way: 1111 of the 4219 integration test files under `modules/apps` keep sibling qualifier methods separate, and 415 of them repeat an identical fixture call at the top of two or more `@Test` methods. So raise it as a preference, weigh it against rule 001, and do not flag a handful of well named scenario methods that each need a fixture of their own. It carries the most weight when the repeated fixture is expensive (an integration test that adds a company, a site, or a depot entry per scenario) or when the methods are numerous or each is a trivial delegation. A test class is never required to make every assertion its own method, and a consolidated test that grows unwieldy is worth splitting back.

**Rationale:** A pile of near identical trivial test methods is grouped only by chance, adjacency and a shared prefix, and adds ceremony without clarity. Scenarios of one method spread across several `@Test` methods cost more than ceremony: each pays for its own fixture, so the suite creates the same expensive objects once per scenario, and the reader has to visit every method to find which ones cover the method under test. One `@Test` per method under test puts them in one place, and the fixture is built once for all of them. But a small number of distinct, well named scenarios reads fine as separate methods, which is why this stays a judgment call.

A violation is a group of trivial `@Test` methods that each only call the same assertion helper with a different argument, as in the first example, or sibling `@Test` methods sharing a `test<MethodUnderTest>` stem that open by building the same fixture, as in the second. A few descriptively named scenario methods with fixtures of their own are not a violation.

**Example:** https://github.com/brianchandotcom/liferay-portal/pull/175426 is the trivial form. Three `@Test` methods (`testNoSuchModelExceptionReturnsNotFound`, `testPrincipalExceptionReturnsNotFound`, `testSecurityExceptionReturnsNotFound`) each call `_assertNotFoundResponse` with a different path. Because they are pure delegations differing only by the path, they should become one `testNotFoundResponse` method that calls a `_testNotFoundResponse` helper three times, once per path.

**Example:** LPD-104558 is the fixture form. `LayoutPageTemplateDepotRolePermissionsContributorTest` covered the subject's one public method with `testGetDepotRolePermissionsWithDesignLibraryMember` and `testGetDepotRolePermissionsWithDesignLibraryRoles`, and each opened by adding a depot entry of its own. They become one `testGetDepotRolePermissions` that adds the depot entry once and passes its group to `_testGetDepotRolePermissionsWithDesignLibraryMember(group)` and `_testGetDepotRolePermissionsWithDesignLibraryRoles(group)`.
